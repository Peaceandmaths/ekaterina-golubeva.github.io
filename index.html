The model exists. The patient never meets it.

Picture a radiologist reading hundreds of images in a day, looking for one small abnormality that could change a patient’s life.

Somewhere, a research group has already trained an algorithm that could help her. It performed well in the paper.

But it never reaches her workstation. It hasn’t been tested on data from other hospitals, nobody has mapped how it would fit into her workflow, and the path through validation, integration and regulation is unclear.

The patient never knows what might have been caught earlier. That distance between a promising model and everyday practice is the problem I want to understand better.

What I’m building

My long-term aim is to make the journey from medical AI research to meaningful clinical adoption easier, faster and more evidence-based.

I’m at an early stage. I’m testing whether a small, practical first step is useful to the people who face this gap every day.

Pilot offer

AI Clinical Readiness Snapshot

“Understand what stands between your medical AI model and real-world clinical use.”

A concise, one-page preliminary assessment for medical AI developers, early-stage startups and radiology teams. It is built from public papers, documentation and whatever you choose to share.

This is an exploratory research service. It is not regulatory certification, and it is not hospital deployment. No patient data is needed, and please don’t send any.

What you receive

  • The clinical problem and intended use case
  • Existing validation evidence
  • Generalizability and dataset limitations
  • Potential clinical workflow challenges
  • Missing evidence and suggested next validation steps

How it works

Three simple steps. The offer is being piloted, so the scope, timing and format are agreed with each person individually.

  1. 1

    Share your AI project

    Send a publication, documentation or a short description of your model. Public or voluntarily shared material only.

  2. 2

    Receive a preliminary assessment

    You get a one-page review of the evidence and the potential gaps between your model and clinical use.

  3. 3

    Discuss possible next steps

    We talk through which validation steps could matter most, and whether further work makes sense.

About me

I’m a PhD researcher in medical imaging AI at the Zurich University of Applied Sciences (ZHAW) in Zürich. I develop and evaluate deep learning models for medical image segmentation, with a focus on intracranial aneurysms.

Working with real clinical imaging data, including collaborations involving Geneva University Hospitals (HUG), has shown me how far a good result in a paper can be from a tool a clinician can rely on. I want to help close that distance. I’m exploring this as a founder-in-the-making, not as an established deployment expert.
